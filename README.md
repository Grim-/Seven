# Seven
Seven is an AI harness for home users. You run a model on your own machine, and Seven is everything around it: a library of your stuff, agents with tools and memory, and a screen that shows you things instead of just talking at you.

Why it exists
A harness for home, not for developers. Most local-model apps are a chat window and a model picker. Seven is the part around the model that makes a small local model useful day to day.
The agent does the grunt work; you get something to act on. Agents handle the hard parts: which tool to use, reasoning it through, reading and writing the library. What reaches you is concrete. A choice is buttons, reasoning is a box you can open and change, a character is a sheet, a plan is a checklist. No vague "thinking…": everything on screen is something you can look at, use or change.
Everything is a kind. Pages, files, chats, agents, themes, days, routines: one concept, so finding, linking, showing and exporting work the same for all of them, and new things slot in as new kinds.
One way to link. @kind:Name, the same for you and for agents, everywhere.
You can do anything an agent can. Agents help; they're never the only way in. Agents act, you decide: permissions, what they see, and what's on are always yours.
Extensible. Add-ons bring new tools, kinds, views, backgrounds and presentations, in plain JavaScript and HTML.
Private by default, not a fortress. It runs locally and your data stays on your machine. The vault encrypts it so other programs can't casually read it. It isn't hardened against a determined attacker and doesn't claim to be: privacy is a given here, not the selling point.
It should feel good to use. Themes, backgrounds, music and voices are part of it. A little goes a long way.
